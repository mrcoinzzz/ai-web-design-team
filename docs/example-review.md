# What a useful review looks like

This is a **fictional example**, not a live-site evaluation or a measured conversion result.

**Brief:** a document-request tool for accountants. Primary action: book a demo. Evidence: desktop/mobile screenshots and a brief confirming the CTA opens a sales contact form. No live browser or analytics.

**Direction:** explain the task the product helps with, make the demo action explicit, and give the mobile hero more breathing room. Retain the logo and restrained palette.

| Priority | Evidence and impact | Proposed change | Verification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High | Hero says “Work smarter.” It doesn't explain the product. This is a comprehension judgment. | Headline: “Collect client documents without the email chase.” Supporting copy: “Send document requests and see what's still outstanding in one place.” Use only if these capabilities are confirmed. | Ask representative visitors to describe the product after viewing the hero. |
| Medium | “Get started” opens a sales contact form according to the brief. The next step is unclear. | Change it to “Book a demo.” Keep the destination and tracking. | Inspect the live destination and tracking after editing. Conversion gains remain a hypothesis. |
| Medium | In the mobile screenshot, the headline fills most of the viewport and the action falls below it. | Reduce the mobile headline scale and hero padding, retaining comfortable button spacing. Tune with actual copy in the rendered page. | Capture the same viewport; inspect wrapping, overflow, and action position. |

**Retain:** the existing identity and simple navigation. Keep account access visible as a secondary action.

**Limits:** keyboard behavior, measured contrast, form submission, analytics, and conversion impact were not tested. No application files were changed.

Real findings should cite actual pages, screenshots, or source locations. The expected shape is evidence, a concrete change, and a check that could establish whether it works.
